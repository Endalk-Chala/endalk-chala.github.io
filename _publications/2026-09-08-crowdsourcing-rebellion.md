This article examines how diaspora-based digital fundraising reshapes insurgent financing and political legitimacy in Ethiopia. Comparing the Tigray People’s Liberation Front (TPLF), Oromo Liberation Army (OLA), and Amhara Fano, it uses an original dataset of contributions to rebel-affiliated crowdfunding campaigns together with social-media narratives from insurgent leaders and diaspora donors.

The analysis identifies three distinct insurgent media-fundraising network types: **distributed** (Fano), **concentrated** (TPLF), and **balanced** (OLA). These patterns show that digital fundraising is not simply a mechanism for raising money; it also structures transnational networks, frames legitimacy, and influences how diaspora communities participate in contemporary civil conflict.

By linking platform affordances, crowdfunding, and insurgent narratives, the article shows how digitally mediated transnational infrastructures shape both the financial and discursive dimensions of rebellion.
